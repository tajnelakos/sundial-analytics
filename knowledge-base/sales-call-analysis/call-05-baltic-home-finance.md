# Call Transcript — Baltic Home Finance

**Date:** July 2026 · **Duration:** ~17 minutes · **Call type:** First discovery call
**Rep:** Anna Kowalski (Sundial Analytics) · **Prospect:** Rasa Petraityte, VP of Mortgage Lending, Baltic Home Finance (regional lender)

---

**Anna:** Rasa, thanks for the time. Tell me about your current setup — what's driving you to look elsewhere?

**Rasa:** We've used Estemate for about twelve years. It's not that it's broken, exactly — it's that everything about it feels like it's from 2014. Our loan officers complain about the interface constantly, and getting support to respond takes days, not hours.

**Anna:** Twelve years is a long relationship — what would actually have to be true for switching to feel worth the disruption, rather than just "it'd be nicer"?

**Rasa:** Honestly, that's the exact question our leadership keeps asking me. My answer so far has been "the UI is bad," which isn't a strong enough business case on its own.

**Anna:** It's not, you're right — "nicer" doesn't survive a budget conversation. What does a slow support ticket actually cost you operationally? Have you quantified that anywhere?

**Rasa:** Not formally. Anecdotally, loan officers route around the tool when it's being difficult, which means valuations sometimes happen outside the system entirely, which is its own risk.

**Anna:** That's a much stronger business case than "the UI is dated" — valuations happening off-system is a real audit and consistency risk, not a convenience complaint. I'd suggest documenting a few concrete instances of that if you can, because that's what actually moves a budget conversation.

**Rasa:** That's a good reframe, I'll pull a few examples from my team. Separately — one thing that's actually a concern, not just a nice-to-have: we have a growing book of business in a couple of markets where I'm not sure your data coverage is as deep as Estemate's. They've been in this region for over a decade; you're newer here.

**Anna:** That's a fair and specific concern, and I'd rather confirm it precisely than reassure you vaguely. Which markets specifically?

**Rasa:** Two smaller regional markets — [Market A] and [Market B] — maybe 15% of our book combined, but growing.

**Anna:** I don't have our exact coverage depth for those two markets memorized down to the comparable-density level, and I'm not going to guess on a call — I'll get you a real answer from our data team on coverage specifically in those two markets, not a generic "we cover most of the region" answer.

**Rasa:** I'd appreciate that. This is genuinely the thing that would stop a deal for us if the coverage isn't there — not a preference, an actual gap.

**Anna:** Understood, and I'd rather find that out now, on this call, framed honestly, than have it surface late in a signed deal. If our coverage is genuinely thin there, I'll tell you that directly rather than paper over it.

**Rasa:** I appreciate that. Assuming the coverage is solid, what's the actual pitch on switching, beyond "it's a better interface"?

**Anna:** Comparable-level audit trail on every valuation, which matters increasingly for exam readiness even if it's not the trigger today. Faster implementation than a legacy migration typically runs. And valuations happening inside the system rather than off it, which closes the exact gap you described with off-system workarounds.

**Rasa:** Okay. Get me the coverage answer for those two markets and I can actually take this seriously internally. Without that, I don't think this conversation goes further, honestly.

**Anna:** Understood — I'll treat that as the single blocking question and get back to you this week, not in a vague "soon."

**Rasa:** Appreciated. Let's reconvene once you have that.
