# Call Transcript — Meadowbrook Financial

**Date:** August 2026 · **Duration:** ~20 minutes · **Call type:** Third call, formal evaluation
**Rep:** Anna Kowalski (Sundial Analytics) · **Prospect:** Robert Yankov, VP of Mortgage Lending, Meadowbrook Financial (regional bank); joined by two members of his data/risk team

---

**Anna:** Robert, thanks for bringing your team in today. My understanding is you're running a structured evaluation across three vendors — us, Bricklane, and ValuAI. Is that right?

**Robert:** Correct. We built a scoring rubric — accuracy, explainability, price, implementation effort, and vendor stability — and we're scoring all three of you the same way, same questions, same evidence bar. I want to be transparent about that rather than have you guess.

**Anna:** I appreciate the transparency, genuinely — that's a better process than most evaluations we're part of. What's the current state of scoring, if you're able to share?

**Robert:** You're currently ahead on explainability and implementation effort. Bricklane's ahead on price, unsurprisingly. ValuAI is scoring competitively on explainability now too, since their audit trail feature is officially live — that's actually shifted since we started this evaluation a few months ago.

**Anna:** That's a fair and current read — I won't pretend ValuAI's GA launch didn't happen, it did, and it changes part of this conversation. Can I ask what evidence your team used to score their explainability specifically — a live demo on your own data, or their prepared materials?

**Analyst (Meadowbrook):** Prepared materials and one demo, not fully live on our own properties yet — we hadn't pushed that far with any of the three vendors equally.

**Anna:** Then I'd suggest holding all three of you to the same higher bar before finalizing that score — a live audit trail walkthrough on a property your team picks, not prepared for the occasion, for us and for them both. If we're going to be evaluated on explainability, I'd rather it be tested rigorously than taken on materials alone, and I'd want the same standard applied to ValuAI.

**Robert:** That's a fair ask, and honestly a good discipline check on our own process — we should be doing that uniformly. Let's do that live, right now, with us, since we're on the call.

**Anna:** *(runs live audit trail on a property the analyst selects)* ...here's the full comparable set and weighting.

**Analyst (Meadowbrook):** This is more granular than what we saw from ValuAI's demo, though to be fair to them, we should run the identical live test before scoring the comparison formally.

**Anna:** Agreed, that's the right call. On price — I know Bricklane's ahead there, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. What I'd ask your team to score alongside price is the cost of a valuation that can't be defended if your regulator asks a hard question — that's not a hypothetical for a bank your size, and it doesn't show up as a line item in a price comparison unless you explicitly build it into the rubric.

**Robert:** That's actually a gap in our current rubric, honestly — we scored price as a standalone line item, not weighted against downstream risk cost. Let me take that back to how we're weighting this.

**Anna:** I'd rather you weight it however your team genuinely believes is right, even if that doesn't favor us — I just want the full cost accounted for somewhere, not omitted by the rubric's structure.

**Robert:** Fair, and appreciated. Vendor stability — briefly, since we're pressed for time — how should we think about evaluating that fairly across three companies of different sizes?

**Anna:** I'd suggest asking each vendor the same specific questions rather than a general "are you stable" — data portability terms if the relationship ends, number of comparable-sized customers live today, and financial runway if you're comfortable sharing it under NDA. Ask Bricklane and ValuAI those same three questions, and score based on actual answers, not company size as a proxy.

**Robert:** That's a good structure, we'll apply it to all three. Okay — next step on our side is running that live audit-trail test with ValuAI to make the explainability comparison fair, then reconvening to finalize scoring.

**Anna:** Sounds good. I'll send our data portability terms and comparable-customer count in writing so that part of the rubric doesn't need to wait on a future call.

**Robert:** Appreciated. This has been a genuinely useful working session, not just a pitch.
