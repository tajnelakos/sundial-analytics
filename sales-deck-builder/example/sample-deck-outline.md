# Sample output

Fictional deal context and outline for [Sundial Analytics](../../knowledge-base.md).

---

## Input

> Deal context: First substantive meeting with a regional bank's Head of Credit Risk. They currently use a legacy valuation tool and a prior vendor (fictional "ValuAI") made explainability claims that reportedly fell apart under scrutiny. Buyer is risk-focused and skeptical of AI hype.

## Output

```
## Deck Outline — Regional bank, first meeting with Head of Credit Risk
Persona: Head of Credit Risk (risk/compliance buyer) · Stage: First substantive meeting

1. The real cost of an unexplainable valuation
   Purpose: Open on the buyer's actual pain (audit exposure), not on our product — this persona tunes out product-first openers.
   Key point: A valuation your auditor can't get an explanation for is a liability, regardless of how accurate it is.

2. What "explainable" means here, specifically
   Purpose: Pre-empt the "we've heard this before" reaction, given the ValuAI history in this deal.
   Key point: Show the actual audit trail structure (comparables used, weighting logic) — concrete, not a claim.

3. Why we're naming this directly: the ValuAI comparison
   Purpose: Named-objection slide — this deal has a specific prior disappointment in play; ignoring it reads as evasive.
   Key point: Invite the skepticism explicitly, then offer a live demo on a real valuation as the resolution, not more slides.

4. Accuracy, measured the way risk teams measure it
   Purpose: This buyer will not take an accuracy claim at face value — show methodology, not just a number.
   Key point: Walk through how accuracy is validated/benchmarked, not just the headline stat.

5. What changes in your existing workflow
   Purpose: Risk buyers are also evaluating operational disruption, even if it's not their primary lens.
   Key point: Minimal workflow disruption — integrates alongside the current process, not a rip-and-replace.

6. What this looks like for your Q3 audit cycle
   Purpose: Ties directly to a real, time-bound stake for this buyer if one is known from discovery.
   Key point: Position implementation timeline against their actual deadline, not a generic "weeks to launch" claim.

7. Next step: technical validation call
   Purpose: Specific, action-based next step rather than a generic close.
   Key point: Propose a live audit-trail walkthrough on one of their own past valuations, scheduled before the meeting ends.
```

Note what's absent: no "About Us" slide (this buyer needs proof, not company background) and no generic feature-tour slide — every slide ties to either the stated pain or the specific objection already in play.
