Key findings

The most common hard-fail category isn't a banned word — it's a stale competitive claim
Stale-competitive-claim (5 instances) outnumbers prohibited-term (3) and beats unverifiable-claim and positioning-overclaim (2 each) individually. Every single instance happened in July or August — zero in September. This is a real reason to weight compliance-rules-reference.md's category 4 (competitive accuracy) as heavily in writer training as the more familiar prohibited-terms list.
Pass rate collapsed after ValuAI's July 2026 GA launch, then recovered sharply after the September staleness sweep
July pass rate: 40% (2/5). August — the month after ValuAI's audit trail feature went GA, before competitive-landscape.md or the battlecards were updated — pass rate dropped to 12.5% (1/8), driven almost entirely by writers still using the pre-GA "we're the only one with an audit trail" framing. September, after the staleness-detection sweep corrected the source docs, pass rate recovered to 71.4% (5/7) — and the stale-competitive-claim category dropped to zero. See the timeline below.
Battlecards and whitepaper excerpts are the riskiest content types — for different, specific reasons
Both sit at a 0% pass rate (n=2 each — a small sample, worth re-checking as more data comes in). Battlecard updates failed specifically on stale-competitive-claim — unsurprising, since a battlecard's entire job is naming what a competitor does or doesn't have. Whitepaper excerpts failed on positioning-overclaim — they're the content type most likely to make a quantitative or methodology claim that needs to trace back to something real. Different content types fail this check for different, predictable reasons, not the same generic mistake.
Recommendation: make this check a required step whenever a battlecard is refreshed, not just for outward-facing marketing copy
Battlecards are internal sales enablement, not public marketing — but this data shows they're exactly as exposed to stale-competitive-claim risk as public content, and arguably more, since they're written to make competitive comparisons by design. battlecard's own refresh cycle already pulls from competitive-landscape.md; running this check as part of that refresh would catch what this log shows slipping through in July–August.
